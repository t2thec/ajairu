# AI Readiness Checklist for UK SMEs

A practical, no-nonsense checklist to help you assess whether your business is genuinely ready to adopt AI. This complements the interactive [AI Readiness Quiz](../tools/ai-readiness-quiz.html) and the full assessment at [ajairu.ai/assessment](https://ajairu.ai/assessment).

## How to use this checklist

Work through each section honestly. Score each item as Yes, Partial, or No. At the end, tally your score to get a rough readiness indicator. This is a starting point, not a verdict. Many businesses score low and that is fine. The point is to know where you stand.

---

## 1. Data foundations

AI is only as good as the data behind it. Before investing in AI, assess your data maturity.

- [ ] Your business data is stored in structured, accessible systems (databases, spreadsheets with consistent schemas, APIs)
- [ ] You know where your critical data lives (no "data is in someone's email" situations)
- [ ] Data is reasonably clean and consistent (no major duplication, formatting is standardised)
- [ ] You have documented what data you collect, where it is stored, and who owns it
- [ ] You can export or query your data without needing a developer for every request
- [ ] Historical data exists (at least 6 to 12 months of records for the process you want to improve)

**Score:** 0-6 items checked

## 2. Clear use case

AI for the sake of AI wastes money. You need a specific problem.

- [ ] You have identified at least one specific, measurable problem AI could help with
- [ ] You can describe the problem in one sentence (e.g. "we spend 15 hours per week manually categorising support tickets")
- [ ] You have a baseline metric for the current process (time, cost, error rate)
- [ ] You have defined what success looks like (e.g. "reduce ticket triage time by 50%")
- [ ] The problem is repetitive and rules-based, or involves pattern recognition
- [ ] You have ruled out simpler solutions (better workflow tool, automation script, process change)

**Score:** 0-6 items checked

## 3. Team and skills

AI adoption needs people who can champion and maintain it.

- [ ] Someone in your team is willing to own the AI initiative (a named person, not "the IT team")
- [ ] That person is comfortable with technology and willing to learn (you do not need a data scientist)
- [ ] Your team is open to changing how they work (AI adoption fails when people resist new tools)
- [ ] You have access to external expertise for the bits you cannot do internally
- [ ] Leadership is supportive and has budget allocated (even a small amount)
- [ ] You have time to dedicate (at least a few hours per week for a pilot)

**Score:** 0-6 items checked

## 4. Technical readiness

- [ ] Your systems can integrate with external APIs (most modern tools can)
- [ ] You have someone who can set up API connections or use no-code integration tools
- [ ] Your data is in a format that can be sent to an AI service (text, numbers, images, structured records)
- [ ] You have a test environment or can run a pilot without risking production systems
- [ ] You understand that AI outputs need human review before being acted upon
- [ ] You have a way to measure whether the AI is actually working (feedback loop)

**Score:** 0-6 items checked

## 5. Governance and risk

UK SMEs have real obligations around data and AI use.

- [ ] You understand which of your data is personal data under UK GDPR
- [ ] You have a lawful basis for processing the data you would send to an AI service
- [ ] You know whether your AI provider processes data in the UK, EEA, or elsewhere
- [ ] You have considered whether AI outputs could introduce bias or discrimination in decisions about customers or staff
- [ ] You have a plan for what happens if the AI gets something wrong (human-in-the-loop, escalation)
- [ ] You have a basic AI usage policy or are willing to create one

**Score:** 0-6 items checked

## 6. Budget and expectations

- [ ] You understand that AI is an ongoing cost, not a one-time purchase
- [ ] You have a rough idea of what API costs will be for your use case
- [ ] You are prepared for the pilot to fail and are willing to pivot or stop
- [ ] You are not expecting AI to replace staff, but to augment them
- [ ] You have a timeline of weeks to months, not days, for seeing initial results
- [ ] You are willing to invest in training your team to use new tools effectively

**Score:** 0-6 items checked

## Scoring

Add up your checked items across all sections (maximum 36).

| Score | Readiness | What to do next |
|-------|-----------|-----------------|
| 30-36 | **Ready** | Pick your highest-impact use case and start a pilot. Talk to [ajairu.ai](https://ajairu.ai) for implementation support. |
| 20-29 | **Nearly ready** | Focus on your weakest section. Usually this is data foundations or governance. Fix those first. |
| 10-19 | **Early stage** | Do not rush into AI. Invest in data, define a clear use case, and build team capability. The [AI Readiness Quiz](../tools/ai-readiness-quiz.html) can help you prioritise. |
| 0-9 | **Not ready yet** | That is completely fine. Focus on getting your data and processes in order first. AI will be far more effective when the foundations are solid. |
