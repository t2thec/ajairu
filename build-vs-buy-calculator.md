# Build vs Buy: A Decision Framework for UK SMEs

A practical guide to deciding whether to build custom software or buy an off-the-shelf solution. This complements the interactive [Build vs Buy Calculator](../tools/build-vs-buy-calculator.html). For bespoke software development support, visit [ajairu.dev](https://ajairu.dev).

## The core question

When you need software to run part of your business, you have three options:

1. **Buy** an off-the-shelf product (SaaS, licensed software)
2. **Build** custom software (in-house or with a development partner)
3. **Hybrid** - buy a platform and customise or extend it

Most SMEs default to buying because it feels safer. But the wrong choice, in either direction, is expensive. This guide helps you think it through properly.

---

## When buying makes sense

Buy when:

- **The problem is common.** If every business like yours has the same need, someone has probably built a good product for it. CRM, accounting, email marketing, project management. These are solved problems.
- **Speed matters more than fit.** You need something working this month, not in six months.
- **Your process follows industry norms.** If your workflow is fairly standard, a configured off-the-shelf product will cover 80%+ of your needs.
- **You have limited technical resources.** No in-house developers and no budget for ongoing development.
- **The total cost of ownership is clearly lower.** Run the numbers (see below).

## When building makes sense

Build when:

- **Your process is your competitive advantage.** If how you operate is what sets you apart, cramming that into a generic tool erodes your edge.
- **Off-the-shelf tools do not fit.** You have tried three products and none of them match your actual workflow. You are paying for features you do not use and still need workarounds.
- **You need deep integration.** Your software needs to talk to niche or legacy systems that off-the-shelf tools do not support.
- **You are paying more in subscriptions than development would cost.** If you are spending GBP 2,000+ per month on SaaS tools that only partially work, custom software may be cheaper within 12 to 18 months.
- **You want to own the asset.** Custom software is a business asset. SaaS subscriptions are an ongoing liability.
- **Compliance or security requires control.** Some regulated industries need full control over data handling, which SaaS may not provide.

## The hybrid option

Often the best answer is neither pure buy nor pure build. Buy a flexible platform (like a low-code tool or a product with a strong API) and build the parts that make your business unique on top of it. This reduces development cost and risk while still giving you the customisation you need.

## The cost comparison framework

### Buying costs

| Cost type | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| Subscription/licence fees | GBP X per user per month |
| Implementation and configuration | Initial setup, data migration, training |
| Integration costs | Connecting to your existing systems |
| Ongoing customisation | Configuration changes as needs evolve |
| Switching cost | If you ever leave (data export, retraining, process redesign) |
| Opportunity cost | Productivity lost to features that do not fit your process |

### Building costs

| Cost type | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| Development (one-time) | Design, build, test, deploy |
| Infrastructure (ongoing) | Hosting, databases, monitoring |
| Maintenance (ongoing) | Bug fixes, security patches, updates |
| Enhancement (ongoing) | New features as your needs change |
| Team or partner cost | In-house developers or external partner |
| Opportunity cost | Time to build vs immediate use of a bought product |

### A rough formula

**3-year buy cost** = (monthly subscription x 36) + setup + integration

**3-year build cost** = development + (monthly maintenance x 36) + infrastructure

If build cost is lower over 3 years and the software fits better, building wins. If buy cost is lower and the fit is acceptable, buying wins. If the numbers are close, fit and flexibility should decide it.

## Decision factors beyond cost

### Fit
How well does the solution match your actual process? A 70% fit off-the-shelf often means 30% of your workflow is manual workarounds. An 85%+ custom fit means your team spends less time fighting the tool.

### Flexibility
Can the solution evolve as your business changes? SaaS products change on the vendor's roadmap, not yours. Custom software changes on yours, but you pay for every change.

### Control
Who owns the data, the workflow, and the roadmap? With SaaS, the vendor owns the product. With custom software, you own it but also own the maintenance burden.

### Speed to value
Buying gets you something this month. Building gets you something better in 3 to 9 months. What is the cost of waiting?

### Risk
Buying risks vendor lock-in, price hikes, and feature changes you cannot control. Building risks project failure, budget overruns, and maintenance debt. Both are real. Assess honestly.

## A simple decision process

1. **Define the problem precisely.** What process needs software, and what does good look like?
2. **Research existing products.** Spend a week evaluating what is available. Do not skip this step.
3. **Score fit honestly.** If existing products cover 85%+ of your needs, buying is likely right.
4. **Estimate 3-year costs** for buy vs build using the framework above.
5. **Assess your team.** Can you maintain custom software, or do you need a development partner?
6. **Consider the hybrid option.** Can you buy a platform and extend it?
7. **Decide and commit.** Indecision is the most expensive option.

## When to revisit the decision

- Your business has grown significantly and the current solution no longer fits
- Subscription costs have risen past the build break-even point
- Your process has changed and the tool no longer matches
- The vendor has been acquired or changed direction
